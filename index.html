Freelancing explained

You sell a skill to clients online, one project at a time

Think of a tailor who sews for many customers and is not employed by one fashion house. A freelancer works the same way, except the customers are online and can be in Lagos, London or Texas. You agree a job and a price, do the work on your laptop, and get paid for that job.

  1. STEP 1

    Learn a skill people pay for

    For example designing a website, a logo or a flyer, or setting up an automation for a business.

  2. STEP 2

    Open a profile

    You join a marketplace such as Fiverr or Upwork and show samples of your work. It works like a shop front.

  3. STEP 3

    Agree the job

    A client finds your service or you apply to their job post. You agree the work, the price and the deadline.

  4. STEP 4

    The client pays first

    The client pays the marketplace before you start. The marketplace holds the money while you work, so you are protected.

  5. STEP 5

    Deliver the work

    You send the finished work through the marketplace. The client checks it and approves.

  6. STEP 6

    Get paid

    The marketplace releases your money after taking its fee. You withdraw it through a payment service such as Payoneer.

What it takes

Freelancing is real work. Your first order can take weeks to arrive, and clients pay for quality, so the skill has to be good before the money comes.

That is why we train the skill first, then the profile, then how to write proposals and talk to clients. Petronix has worked this way with clients on Fiverr and Upwork for years, and we teach what we do every day.

Network marketing explained

A business built on products, customers and a team you train

Some companies do not sell through shops and adverts. They sell through independent distributors who recommend the products to people they know. That is network marketing.

How it works

You become a distributor of a company's products. You earn in two ways. The first is from products you sell to customers. The second is from the sales of the people you bring in, train and support, because the company pays you for building and leading that team.

Freelancing pays you for your own hours. A trained team keeps selling when you are in a lecture or at your PPA. We teach both because each one covers what the other lacks.

The lessons also transfer. Speaking to strangers, presenting, following up and leading people are the same skills that win freelance clients and job interviews.

Straight talk

Network marketing is not a salary and it is not quick money. Income comes from real products that customers buy and use. It does not come from collecting fees from recruits, and any scheme that pays only for recruiting should be avoided.

People who learn the skills and work consistently build something. People who sign up and do nothing earn nothing. We will tell you that on the first day.

Questions

What people ask first

Is it really free?

The seminar, the training and the mentoring are free, and we charge no registration fee. Network marketing is a required part of the programme, and at the seminar we explain exactly what registering as a distributor involves before you commit to anything.

Do I need a laptop?

A laptop helps, especially for web design. You can begin learning AI skills, graphic design and freelance selling with a smartphone and move to a laptop later.

Will this clash with my lectures or my PPA?

No. You train around your own timetable. Our Ondo office opens Monday to Friday, and you come in the hours you are free.

How soon will I earn?

Nobody can promise that. It depends on how well you learn the skill and how consistently you apply. We can promise to teach you properly and review your work until you get there.

Do I have to do both freelancing and network marketing?

Yes. The programme has two parts and every member does both. Freelancing gives you income from your own skill, and network marketing teaches you to sell and lead a team. If you want only one of them, this programme is not for you.

Is network marketing a pyramid scheme?

A pyramid scheme pays people for recruiting and has no real product. Network marketing pays on products that customers buy and use. Ask any company two questions: is there a real product, and are people paid on sales and not on sign-up fees?
